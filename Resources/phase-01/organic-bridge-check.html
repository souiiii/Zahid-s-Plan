Organic bridge readiness check

20 original prerequisite questions, written for this phase. These are not CBSE PYQs. Use the selected NCERT XI Unit 8 passages and the foundation video to learn; attempt this check with those closed. About 35–45 minutes, then check the answers. A mistake identifies a specific concept to repair, not a reason to restart all of Class 11.

  1. Name CH3CH2CH2Br using IUPAC nomenclature.
  2. Draw the condensed structure of 2-methylpropan-1-ol.
  3. Identify the functional group in each: CH3CHO, CH3COCH3, CH3COOH, CH3NH2, CH3OCH3.
  4. Are CH3CH2OH and CH3OCH3 identical compounds or functional-group isomers? Explain using their molecular formula.
  5. State the hybridisation of each carbon atom in CH3CHO.
  6. Count sigma and pi bonds in ethene and ethyne.
  7. Explain why both OH− and NH3 can act as nucleophiles, although NH3 is neutral.
  8. Why can BF3 and CH3+ act as electrophiles?
  9. Mark the partially positive and partially negative ends of the C–Br bond in CH3Br. Which atom can a nucleophile attack?
  10. Show the **formal heterolytic cleavage** of the C–Br bond in CH3Br, assigning the electron pair to Br. This is an electron-bookkeeping exercise, not a claim that a free methyl carbocation forms in an actual substitution.
  11. Show homolytic cleavage of Cl–Cl. How is the electron allocation different from Q10?
  12. In the protonation of NH3 by H+, from where to where should the curved electron-pair arrow point?
  13. Arrange methyl, primary ethyl, secondary isopropyl and tertiary tert-butyl carbocations in increasing stability. Restrict the comparison to these simple alkyl carbocations.
  14. Draw the two equivalent resonance contributors of CH3COO−. Do any atom positions change?
  15. Which is the stronger acid, CH3COOH or ClCH2COOH? Give the electronic reason.
  16. Why is phenol more acidic than ethanol? Think about the stability of the conjugate bases.
  17. Why is methylamine more basic than ammonia in the standard aqueous comparison? Do not extend this one comparison into a universal ranking of all amines.
  18. Classify each reaction as substitution, elimination, addition or oxidation: (a) CH3Br + OH− → CH3OH + Br−; (b) ethanol → ethene + water on acid-catalysed heating; (c) ethene + HBr → bromoethane; (d) ethanol → ethanal using an oxidising agent.
  19. If one substituent is at carbon 1 of benzene, which positions are ortho, meta and para to it?
  20. For the electron-pair movement in CH3Br + OH− → CH3OH + Br−, draw an arrow from an oxygen lone pair to carbon and another from the C–Br bond to bromine. Explain what each arrow means. Full SN1/SN2 kinetics and stereochemistry belong to the next chapter.
Reveal answers after attempting

Answer check — open after attempting

  1. 1-Bromopropane.
  2. (CH3)2CHCH2OH. The –OH carbon must be carbon 1.
  3. Aldehyde; ketone; carboxylic acid; amine; ether, respectively.
  4. Functional-group isomers: both have formula C2H6O, with different connectivity and functional groups.
  5. The methyl carbon is sp3; the carbonyl carbon is sp2.
  6. Ethene: 5 sigma and 1 pi. Ethyne: 3 sigma and 2 pi.
  7. Each has an available lone pair that can be donated to an electron-deficient centre. A nucleophile need not carry a negative charge.
  8. They have an electron-deficient centre with an incomplete octet and can accept an electron pair.
  9. Carbon δ+–Br δ−. The nucleophile attacks the carbon; Br is more electronegative.
  10. CH3Br → CH3+ + Br−, with both bonding electrons allocated to Br. Use a full curved arrow from the bond to Br.
  11. Cl–Cl → Cl· + ·Cl. Each atom receives one bonding electron; show fishhook arrows if drawing the movement.
  12. From the nitrogen lone pair to H+. Arrows show electron movement, not the direction in which a positive ion travels.
  13. CH3+ < CH3CH2+ < (CH3)2CH+ < (CH3)3C+. Alkyl groups stabilise positive charge through electron donation and hyperconjugation. Resonance-stabilised ions require a separate comparison.
  14. CH3–C(=O)–O− ↔ CH3–C(–O−)=O. The two oxygen atoms exchange the formal double-bond/charge roles; atom positions do not change. The real ion is a resonance hybrid.
  15. ClCH2COOH. Chlorine withdraws electron density by its −I effect and stabilises the conjugate base.
  16. Phenoxide distributes negative charge through resonance; ethoxide lacks comparable resonance delocalisation into an aromatic ring.
  17. The methyl group donates electron density through its +I effect, increasing the availability of the nitrogen lone pair in this comparison. Solvation also matters when comparing different amines in water.
  18. (a) Substitution; (b) elimination; (c) addition; (d) oxidation.
  19. Ortho: 2 and 6. Meta: 3 and 5. Para: 4. Recognition only; chapter-specific directing rules come later.
  20. The first arrow shows oxygen donating an electron pair to form a C–O bond. The second shows the C–Br electron pair going to Br, producing Br−. Carbon must not be drawn with five completed covalent bonds in the product.

Readiness: aim for comfortable independent answers on structures/naming, nucleophile versus electrophile, electron-pair arrows and simple inductive/resonance reasoning. A useful working checkpoint is about 16/20, with no persistent confusion in those essentials. This is a planning checkpoint, not an exam-score prediction. Reattempt incorrect items after a short targeted repair. Do not delay Haloalkanes to master advanced mechanisms or all Hydrocarbons.
