Before Continuity: the essentials

Unscheduled catch-up for 24–25 October. The formal planner starts 26 October. These are original prerequisite checks, not CBSE PYQs. Use only the sections that expose a gap.

Work in this order: read a rule → cover the example → solve the matching checks → reveal answers. Ask for help with the exact failed item. A readiness check guides repair; it does not replace the Class 12 chapter.

1. Algebra you will use

Know (a²−b²)=(a−b)(a+b), simple factorisation and cancelling factors. Cancelling does not remove the original domain restriction. For example, (x²−4)/(x−2)=x+2 only for x≠2. For a square root difference, multiply by its conjugate: (√(x+1)−1)/x=1/(√(x+1)+1) for x≠0.

Exponent rules: xᵃxᵇ=xᵃ⁺ᵇ; (xᵃ)ᵇ=xᵃᵇ on the relevant domain. For logarithms of positive arguments: ln(ab)=ln a+ln b and ln(aᵏ)=k ln a. A logarithm needs a positive argument.

2. Functions and graphs

f(a) means substitute a into the function. A piecewise rule tells you which formula to use on each interval. Sketch y=x, x², 1/x and |x|; understand what happens near a point. A denominator cannot be zero; a real square root needs a nonnegative argument. Composite f(g(x)) means do g first.

3. Trigonometry needed here

Use radians in limits and differentiation: 180°=π radians. Recall sin²x+cos²x=1, tan x=sin x/cos x, sin 0=0 and cos 0=1. Know sine/cosine at 0, π/6, π/4, π/3 and π/2. Recall the principal ranges of inverse trig; use the existing inverse-trig revision card when needed. Do not add a complete Class 11 trigonometry course.

4. Limits before continuity

A limit asks what values a function approaches near a point; f(a) asks its value at the point. For LHL approach from smaller x; for RHL approach from larger x. A two-sided limit exists when both sides agree. Try substitution first; 0/0 is a cue to simplify, not an answer. Know limx→0 sin x/x=1 in radians and limx→0 (1−cos x)/x=0. Continuity will combine the limit with f(a).

Readiness check · solutions closed

  1. Simplify (x²−9)/(x−3), stating the excluded value.
  2. Simplify (√(1+x)−1)/x for x≠0.
  3. For f(x)=2x+1 and g(x)=x², find f(g(2)) and g(f(2)).
  4. State the real domain of √(x−1)/(x−3).
  5. If f(x)=x+1 for x<1 and f(x)=3 for x≥1, find f(1), LHL and RHL at 1.
  6. Convert 30°, 90° and 180° to radians.
  7. State sin(π/6), cos(π/3), sin(π/4) and cos(π/2).
  8. Find limx→3 (x²−9)/(x−3), and explain whether the original expression has a value at 3.
  9. Find limx→0 sin(3x)/x in radians.
  10. Find limx→0 |x|/x from each side. Does the two-sided limit exist?
  11. Simplify ln(x²) for x>0, and state the domain of ln(x−2).
  12. Sketch y=|x| and y=x². Explain why the first has a corner at 0 while the second does not.

  2. 1/(√(1+x)+1), on its real domain with x≠0.
  3. 9 and 25.
  4. [1,3) ∪ (3,∞).

  6. π/6, π/2, π.
