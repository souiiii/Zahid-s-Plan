**Monday, 2 November–Thursday, 31 December 2026**

Aim: finish the remaining theory syllabus with substantial written practice by **20 December**, then consolidate through **31 December**. These are weekly progress targets, with no daily timetable or fixed slots. Dates can move slightly when a chapter needs more work.

Starting point, using the chapter numbering in the supplied NCERT books:

- Physics: Chapters 1–3 completed, through Current Electricity.
- Chemistry: Chapter 1, Solutions, completed.
- Maths: Chapters 1–5 completed, through Continuity and Differentiability.

“Completed” initially means studied; a short diagnostic will establish whether earlier chapters need repair. The plan covers PCM theory. English, other subjects, schoolwork and practical preparation need separate capacity.

**Workload assumption**

Allow about **42 focused hours per full week across PCM**, excluding breaks: approximately 13 Physics, 15 Chemistry and 14 Maths. That is roughly 6 hours averaged across seven days, or 7 hours across six days. This is a demanding preparation workload, feasible only if school and other commitments leave that capacity. It is not an assumption that Zahid can do this on top of a full school day indefinitely.

**Practice that counts towards completion**

Use the same progression for each topic: learn the idea, attempt NCERT examples independently, solve relevant in-text/exercise questions, then add selected Exemplar and CBSE past-paper questions. Cover each distinct syllabus-relevant question type before adding more repetitions of an easy type. Include relevant miscellaneous exercises in Maths.

For a weak type, study the correction, solve a fresh similar question unaided, and revisit it later. Keep a short error list identifying the reason: concept, method, recall, calculation or presentation. Reading a solution does not count as solving it.

- **Physics:** conceptual explanations, numericals, graphs, diagrams and only the derivations required by the syllabus. Write formulas, substitutions, units and conclusions clearly.
- **Chemistry:** numerical setups; NCERT facts and reasoning; balanced reactions with conditions; naming, products, conversions and distinguishing tests. Revisit organic reactions across subsequent weeks rather than learning them once.
- **Maths:** write complete solutions without looking at worked steps. Practise choosing the method in mixed sets, checking domains/limits and drawing graphs where needed.

Include MCQs, assertion–reason where applicable, short/long answers and case-based questions across the weekly practice. Use a short chapter check after a chapter or related group. Revisit earlier material every week within the listed practice hours: start with Physics Chapters 1–3, Chemistry Solutions and Maths Chapters 1–5, then rotate in newly completed chapters. Do not postpone all revision until December.

Mark a chapter **studied → practised → checked → revised**. Keep it at “practised” if independent questions still reveal unresolved gaps. The weekly table records intended progress; it is not evidence that a chapter has been mastered.

**If progress is slower**

First use the flexible hours, then reduce duplicate PYQs and optional Exemplar questions. Preserve NCERT coverage, correction time and practice of unfamiliar types. If a chapter still needs several extra hours, carry it forward explicitly and revise the finish date. A substantial backlog or markedly less than 42 hours a week makes extensive practice plus a December finish less realistic; do not compensate by racing through lectures. The December target is strong preparation, not a guaranteed score.
