# CBSE Class 12 PCM — rough topic/date map

Updated: 22 October 2026. Target: finish the first learning-and-practice pass by **31 December 2026**, assuming the 2026–27 session and 2027 boards. Available time: **5–6 focused hours daily for PCM combined**. The full revision phase will be planned separately after December.

**Planning authority:** this roadmap is the source of truth for subsequent phases until syllabus coverage is complete. Phase resource pools and calendar plans inherit its scope and sequence. Phase 1 starts **23 October**, as confirmed by the student; no study on 22 October is assumed. Its resources are recorded in [the Phase 1 resource pool](Resources/phase-01/RESOURCE_POOL.md).

**Starting point:** Maths — Relations and Functions, Inverse Trigonometric Functions, Matrices and Determinants covered. Physics — Electric Charges and Fields, Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance covered. Chemistry — only basic organic familiarity; do not assume a secure Class 11 organic foundation. All 10 Class 12 chemistry chapters remain.

**Organic progresses slowly from the minimum necessary base.** Chemistry has two small parallel tracks so organic gets almost two months without pushing all physical and inorganic chemistry into the end of December. All columns run alongside each other. Each block includes learning, immediate question practice and short returns to older material. The two chemistry tracks share chemistry's time allocation; they are not two full additional subjects. Start with roughly two hours of chemistry daily, usually about 75 minutes for organic and 45 minutes for the other track. Use the remaining time for maths, physics and continuous revision. Actual time must move toward whichever subject is lagging.

**Minimum organic base — about 7–10 days initially, with small additions when needed:**

- Read and draw structures: carbon valency, functional groups, basic IUPAC naming, sigma/pi bonds and essential hybridisation.
- Understand polarity, inductive effect and resonance well enough to explain common stability, acidity/basicity and reactivity comparisons. Add hyperconjugation only where it helps a prescribed explanation.
- Recognise nucleophiles, electrophiles, bond breaking and the common intermediates used in Class 12 mechanisms, especially carbocations.
- Recognise substitution, elimination, addition, oxidation and reduction; read reagents, conditions and products.
- Recall only the alkene and benzene reaction ideas needed for the next Class 12 topic; introduce directing effects and basic stereochemistry when the chapter needs them.

Move into Haloalkanes when simple structures, electron movement and basic reactivity make sense. Do not wait to master all Class 11 organic chemistry. No separate full Hydrocarbons course, Class 11 purification/elemental-analysis theory or advanced entrance-exam mechanism catalogue is assigned. Prescribed Class 12 mechanisms still need to be understood.

**Marks-focused filter:** current CBSE theory scope → the corresponding NCERT concepts/examples/exercises → selected current-syllabus board questions. Prioritise standard numericals, required derivations, diagrams, explanations, reaction conditions, conversions and distinguishing tests. Choose representative questions and repeat weak types; stop accumulating equivalent questions once they can be solved independently. Do not treat lecture completion as chapter completion.

Exclude out-of-syllabus theory, entrance-exam extensions and derivations explicitly not required by CBSE. A low past-paper count does not establish that a current-syllabus topic cannot be asked. Keep all examinable chapters, while allocating depth and practice by official scope, marks and personal difficulty. Chemistry's formative-only additions get no dedicated written-board theory blocks; practical requirements remain separate. In particular, Surface Chemistry can still matter for practical assessment. [Current chemistry syllabus](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart2/Chemistry_SecP2_2026-27.pdf).

**Continuous revision stays:** use roughly 45–75 minutes within the same daily 5–6 hours for older material, adjusting on heavier days. Revisit material at approximately 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, or sooner after failed recall. Start with the already-covered chapters. For organic, repeatedly retrieve reaction links, reagents, conditions and reasons while learning later chapters. Each return starts with recall or questions, then checking the relevant maintained chapter note. This is maintenance during learning; there is no dedicated late-December revision phase.

**AI-managed notes, later:** the detailed planner will link each revision task to one living chapter note containing concise concepts, formulae/reactions, selected question references and personal mistakes. AI will append progress and correct notes when study results are reported. No detailed timetable or chapter-note system is created at this rough-roadmap stage.
